# Reading Strategy Coach Project Brief

# Purpose

Reading Strategy Coach is an AI-powered mobile learning application designed to help learners improve reading comprehension, vocabulary acquisition, reading fluency, and strategic reading.

The project addresses the need for structured reading practice that combines explicit strategy instruction, interactive activities, progress feedback, and personalized support in one learner-focused experience.

---

# Vision

The long-term vision is to provide an accessible and engaging reading coach that helps learners understand how they read, practice effective strategies, build confidence, and become more independent readers.

The application should adapt its guidance to learner progress while preserving clear educational goals and meaningful human oversight.

---

# Objectives

- Improve reading comprehension as measured through learner performance on reading activities.
- Develop the learner's ability to select and apply appropriate reading strategies.
- Enhance vocabulary acquisition and retention through contextual practice.
- Improve reading fluency through repeated and guided reading activities.
- Increase learner motivation and continued participation through gamification.
- Provide personalized AI support based on learner activity and progress.

# Success Criteria

Project success will be measured by:

- Demonstrated improvement in reading comprehension assessments.
- Demonstrated use of appropriate reading strategies during learning activities.
- Improvement in vocabulary acquisition and retention.
- Improvement in reading fluency measures where applicable.
- Learner completion, return, and engagement rates.
- Successful delivery of relevant AI feedback and recommendations.
- Reliable completion of the first-release learning workflows.
- Positive usability feedback from representative target users.

# Risks

- AI feedback may be inaccurate, inconsistent, or unsuitable for a learner's level.
- Reading content may not align with target proficiency levels or learning objectives.
- Gamification may encourage activity completion without improving learning outcomes.
- Learner engagement may decline after initial use.
- Progress measures may not accurately represent reading development.
- Privacy requirements may limit learner data collection and personalization.
- Pending architecture decisions may affect first-release scope or schedule.
- Offline support and synchronization may introduce data consistency risks.
- Dependence on external services may affect availability, cost, or performance.
